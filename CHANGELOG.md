# Changelog

## 1.2.0

- **Flights start when you've spawned, not in the menu.** VBuddy now knows the simulator's menu, hangar, loading screen and replays from flying (the camera state, the "Sim" event, MSFS's placeholder positions, and a position that doesn't move at speed). A flight starts once the aircraft has settled where it was put; a jump of more than 20 nm starts a new one. Replays no longer count as landings and no longer move the end of the flight, and a "flight" that never went anywhere is left out of the list.
- **Older flight records are tidied once:** flights that began at the menu placeholder (0°N 90°E) or in the MSFS 2024 hangar get their real start where the telemetry is still there, replayed landings are set aside (not deleted), and menu stretches are hidden. Flights flown on their SimBrief plan now show up in the Atlas.
- **The SimBrief plan is loaded when you connect to VATSIM**, and again when a flight starts and every couple of minutes until takeoff. It's tied to the flight only if it starts where the flight began, and the record keeps its callsign, route, planned distance and time (shown next to what you flew).
- **The map trail starts once you've spawned and connected to VATSIM** (straight away when vPilot isn't linked to VBuddy), belongs to the flight in progress, and starts again for a new flight. Your SimBrief route is drawn as a dashed line.
- **Weather on the map:** rain radar from RainViewer, and clouds from OpenWeatherMap with a free key pasted into the VBuddy window (the key stays on the PC; the PC fetches the tiles).
- **Notifications have their own page** (the bell), with a main switch and new options: takeoff, top of climb, passing 10,000 ft, approaching the destination, at the gate, flight plan loaded, the simulator stopping mid-flight, and the simulator pausing itself. In the app they show as short notes.
- **Notifications fixed on iPhone:** a push that arrived while the app was open wasn't shown, and Safari takes the permission away from a site after a few of those. Pushes are now always shown, and the bridge doesn't send them to a device that has the app on screen. A phone paired again is linked to its notifications by itself, devices that are no longer paired are dropped, and every notification sent is logged.
- **Airline colours:** connect to VATSIM as QTR123 and the app turns Qatar burgundy, IGO… IndiGo blue and white, UAE… Emirates green, white and black, and so on for about 100 airlines, in light and dark mode. A nameplate on the Live page shows the airline, callsign, plan and distance to go. Put your own logos in %APPDATA%\VBuddy\logos (QTR.png). Switch it off under Settings → Appearance.
- **Screenshots:** Systems → Sim view → *Take screenshot* grabs the sim window at full size, keeps it on the PC (%APPDATA%\VBuddy\screenshots) and saves it to your phone.
- **Inbox is now Comms.**
- **The Live page no longer greys out while data is coming in:** it compared the PC's clock with the phone's, so a phone a few seconds off made it look stale.

## 1.1.1

- **PMDG 737 and 777 set themselves up.** VBuddy finds the aircraft in your Community and Official folders (also when they're linked from another drive) and copies its SDK header into %APPDATA%\VBuddy\sdk, and copies it again when an aircraft update changes it. It also adds the [SDK] lines that turn on PMDG's data and CDU screens (EnableDataBroadcast, EnableCDUBroadcast.0/.1, and .2 on the 777) to the aircraft's 737_Options.ini / 777_Options.ini, with a backup the first time. This runs when VBuddy starts and on every aircraft change, because PMDG makes a fresh options file when the aircraft is reinstalled or updated. When VBuddy has just turned the data on, the app says to reload the aircraft.

## 1.1.0

- **iniBuilds A340 / A350 / A380 get the Fenix-style FCU screen** under Controls (knobs, windows, push/pull, AP1/AP2, A/THR, LOC, APPR, SPD/MACH, HDG-V/S TRK-FPA, METRIC, FD), driven through the aircraft's input events. No EFIS column (its baro and ND aren't wired yet).
- **iniBuilds buttons and push/pull are pressed once:** VBuddy now reads where each button's input event rests and changes it once, instead of "1 then 0", which on buttons resting at 1 was a double press (APPR armed and disarmed again) and on others did nothing.
- **iniBuilds V/S window follows the aircraft:** the A350 keeps its V/S in one of the slot variables (AUTOPILOT VERTICAL HOLD VAR:1-3); VBuddy shows whichever one moves.

- **iniBuilds A340, A350 and A380 controls:** the FCU buttons (AP, FD, A/THR, LOC, APPR), knob turns and push/pull, and the exterior lights now go through the aircraft's own input events (AIRLINER_FCU_* / AIRLINER_MCU_* / AIRLINER_LIGHTS_EXT_*, as found in the aircraft scans) instead of the standard events these aircraft ignore. Which way and how far a knob moves per step is learned on first use by watching the window. The Airbus FCU in the app gains a LOC button.
- **The aircraft's input-event list is asked for again until it arrives:** right after an aircraft loads, SimConnect often refuses the list ("ERROR ... list of the aircraft's input events" in the log), and VBuddy never asked again, so input-event aircraft (iniBuilds, iFly) got no controls.

- **PMDG 737 and 777 CDU in the app (Systems → CDU):** the CDU screen as the aircraft draws it (colours, small font, reverse video), the line select keys beside their lines, the function keys, numbers and letters, the EXEC light and MSG/OFST/FAIL/CALL (DSPY on the 777) lights. Left and right CDU (and centre on the 777). Type into the scratchpad from the phone keyboard, or a computer keyboard (Backspace = CLR, Enter = EXEC). Read the way PMDG's own CDU sample reads it; keys are pressed and released like PMDG's sample presses a switch. Needs `EnableCDUBroadcast.0=1` and `.1=1` (and `.2=1` on the 777) under [SDK] in the aircraft's options .ini; the app says so if they're missing.

- **Airbus aircraft get an Airbus FCU in Controls:** SPD/MACH, HDG, ALT and V/S windows with push/pull, and FD, AP, A/THR and APPR buttons, instead of the Boeing MCP buttons. No course window on an Airbus or on the PMDG 777 (neither has a course knob).
- **Study airliners say plainly that they can't be operated from outside:** the FSLabs A321neo (and ToLiss, iniBuilds A300/A310) keep their cockpit private; Cobalt can't operate them either. Their autopilot and light controls are switched off with that explanation, while the simulator controls (pause, view, flaps etc.) stay available.

- **PMDG MCP windows are set directly:** speed, Mach, heading, altitude, vertical speed and course now use the SDK's own "set" events (EVT_MCP_HDG_SET and friends), so the value lands exactly where it was asked instead of overshooting or creeping back. Turning the knob is kept as a fallback, and it now waits for the aircraft's data to catch up before counting clicks.

- **PMDG knobs and buttons click the way PMDG's sample does:** MCP knobs now turn with the mouse wheel (as PMDG_NG3_ConnectionTest.cpp slews the heading selector) and buttons send the press and then the release. VBuddy also finds the SDK files in the source folder's `sdk` folder, and when it can't find them the app says to copy them into %APPDATA%\VBuddy\sdk.

- **PMDG 737 / 777 controls follow PMDG's own SDK sample:** events are now sent the way PMDG_NG3_ConnectionTest.cpp sends them ("#<event number>" at the highest priority with GROUPID_IS_PRIORITY), instead of writing PMDG's control area without waiting for it to clear (which could lose knob clicks). The aircraft's data is read as one block, as in PMDG's sample. The simulator's standard events are sent at the highest priority too (as PMDG's sample and Cobalt do). The 737's landing lights (L/R RETRACT and FIXED) are found, the 777's missing course knob is no longer reported (it has none), and VBuddy also looks for the SDK files in %APPDATA%\VBuddy\sdk.

## 1.0.0
First release.

- **More aircraft controls from Cobalt** (second pass, every control function in Cobalt Bridge 1.2.5 listed with REA and compared):
  - Airbus FCU knobs can be pushed (managed) and pulled (selected) from the autopilot panel: FlyByWire with its own FCU events, the default A320neo with the simulator's slot-index events, the Fenix with its knob counters; each is checked against the managed dot.
  - FlyByWire: AP1, A/THR, LOC and APPR lights and the managed dots are read from FlyByWire's own FCU (it doesn't copy them into the standard variables), so buttons now confirm properly.
  - Setting speed, heading or V/S first selects the knob (as Cobalt does), so an Airbus in managed mode accepts the new value.
  - Negative values (V/S -1500) are sent the way SimConnect expects; before, they could fail to send.

- **Aircraft controls work the way Cobalt's do** (taken from Cobalt Bridge 1.2.5's own control tables, nothing invented):
  - Fenix A320: lights (beacon, strobe, nav/logo, landing, nose/taxi), seat belts, probe heat, parking brake, speed brake arm and APU (master, then START after 1.5 s) now move the Fenix's own overhead switches instead of the simulator's standard events, and the Systems page shows the Fenix's real switch positions. Each switch is read back to confirm it moved.
  - Fenix FCU: written directly through SimConnect first (MSFS 2020 SU12 and later, MSFS 2024), with MobiFlight only as the fallback for older simulator builds.
  - Fenix radios: COM frequencies are dialled on the Fenix radio panel itself (outer knob 1 MHz a click, inner knob one channel a click, 8.33 or 25 kHz spacing worked out from the first click), and transfer presses the panel's XFER key. The Fenix ignores the simulator's frequency events.
  - Aircraft are recognised in Cobalt's order; the default A320neo (Asobo and iniBuilds' version for MSFS 2024) uses the standard events; study airliners (iniBuilds A300/A310, ToLiss, FSLabs) now say plainly that values must be set on their FCU.
  - FlyByWire LVL CHG pushes the FCU altitude knob; iFly 737 MAX buttons use Cobalt's input-event names.
  - Engines can be started and stopped one at a time (fuel valve and starter) under Systems.
  None of this has been tried in the real simulator yet.

- **Automatic updates:** VBuddy checks for a new version when it starts and every 6 hours, downloads it in the background (but waits while the simulator is running, so it never competes with scenery streaming), checks the download against the release's SHA-256 list, and shows it under Settings → Updates in the VBuddy window with what's new. Install update asks Windows for permission, updates silently and starts VBuddy again. The home network edition gets the home network installer. Turn checks off with VBUDDY_NO_UPDATES=1. Updates come from the releases of github.com/Ravishankar642/Vbuddy-web. Based on how Cobalt's bridge updates itself.

- **ChasePlane views from your phone:** the Sim view page lists the views you saved in ChasePlane (your aircraft's views, and the airport views where you are) as buttons; tap one and the simulator switches to it. VBuddy talks to ChasePlane's own local connection on this PC (port 8652), the same way the open-source ComSpotter tool does (github.com/sw-jannik/comspotter, MIT), so there are no shortcuts to set up. Needs ChasePlane for MSFS 2024 running. Not yet tried with a real ChasePlane.

- **Live sim view (Systems → Sim view):** see the simulator window on your phone or tablet, about three pictures a second, at home or through the cloud relay, with the camera buttons (cockpit, outside, drone, look around, zoom) underneath. Works the way Cobalt's sim view does: the PC only captures while someone is watching, skips a picture if the previous one isn't finished, sends at most 1024 x 576 (JPEG quality 55), and drops pictures for a phone whose connection can't keep up instead of queueing them. Only this PC and paired devices can watch. Unlike Cobalt it never shows your whole screen when the simulator window isn't found; it says so instead. On mobile data it uses roughly 10-20 MB a minute. Not yet tried on a real MSFS window.

- **The installer opens the port by itself:** it always opens TCP 4789 in Windows Firewall for every network type (home Wi-Fi is often set to Public), allows VBuddy's own node.exe, and removes any earlier Block rule for it. The firewall tick box is gone.
- **Two editions of the installer:** `pnpm package:both` makes `VBuddy-Setup-<version>.exe` (with the Cloud relay switch for use away from home) and `VBuddy-Local-Setup-<version>.exe` (home network edition: no relay at all, the switch is hidden, the PC never connects to any VBuddy server, phones connect on the same Wi-Fi). Use `pnpm package:local` for the home edition alone. Installing one over the other switches editions and keeps your flights and settings.
- **Fenix EFB, full screen:** the full-screen EFB page has a "‹ VBuddy" button at the top left that returns to the app.

- **Fenix EFB inside VBuddy, at home and away:** Systems has a new EFB tab (and the PC window an EFB page) that shows the Fenix external EFB from your PC. VBuddy passes the EFB through its own address, so it works on your phone over the cloud relay as well, not only on your home network. It opens only on the PC and on paired devices. The EFB is looked for on port 8083 (change with `--efb-port` or `VBUDDY_EFB_PORT`). To use it away from home, update the relay on your cloud VM (the relay now also carries POST requests and binary data for the EFB; run deploy/relay/setup.sh again with the new relay build). Not yet tried against the real Fenix EFB, so some of its pages may need follow-up fixes.

- **New VBuddy window on the PC:** a sidebar with Connect (addresses, pairing code, cloud relay, paired devices), Live map, Flights, Systems, Atlas, Activity and Settings. Live map, Flights, Systems and Atlas are the app itself, inside the window. Activity lists what the bridge has been doing (with a problems-only filter). Settings has: reconnect to MSFS, pause the simulator later (at a time, or before top of descent, held on the PC), start VBuddy when you sign in to Windows, install or update the vPilot plugin, and your SimBrief and Hoppie details. The window opens larger (1180 x 780). Traffic planes on the map are bigger.

- **My flights and Atlas:** plan the flights you want to fly (from, to, aircraft, date, notes; change or delete them later, kept on your PC). Every flown flight now shows where it left from and arrived at, how far it went and how long it took; tap one to see its details and its route drawn on the map. Atlas totals your flights, hours, distance, airports and countries, lists the airports you have visited and the aircraft you fly most. Airports come from the bundled OurAirports list (public domain). Only flights flown on the route of a filled-in SimBrief plan are logged in the Atlas (the plan's departure and arrival must match where you actually took off and landed); other flights stay in My flights, marked as not logged. A flight's distance is worked out when it ends and kept, so totals stay right after the detailed route is cleared (telemetry is still kept for 24 hours by default).

- **Systems page reads every aircraft the way Cobalt does:** the same simulator variables, read in three groups (engines every few frames, autopilot, fuel, radios and the rest a few times a second, doors, warnings and weather once a second). The main engine gauge follows the engine type (N1 for jets, torque and propeller RPM for turboprops, RPM for pistons). Lights that only show in the aircraft's "lights on" states (many Airbus) now count, the squawk is decoded correctly, and the Fenix and FlyByWire read their FCU targets, managed dots, mode lights and switches from their own cockpit variables. New Overview with instruments and flight phase.

- **New app layout:** a full-screen map with a draggable bottom sheet and a floating tab bar (Flights, Systems, Inbox, Atlas, Search), a bridge status pill, round map buttons (appearance, traffic, ATC sectors, follow) and a settings button. On a tablet or PC the sheet is a side panel. Same burgundy look, light and dark.

- **Systems tab:** a live page of the aircraft: engines (N1, N2, EGT, fuel flow, oil, vibration), fuel, autopilot, surfaces, radios and squawk, electrical and hydraulics, pressurization, doors, lights, warnings and the weather around you. Read once a second from the simulator; values an aircraft does not report show a dash.

- **New look:** burgundy brand theme with **light and dark mode** (button in the top bar: same as device / light / dark; the map follows it). Flight tab redesigned around a hero card (altitude, airspeed, vertical speed, ground speed, heading dial); bottom tab bar on phones, side menu on tablets and desktops; Manrope and Barlow Condensed fonts are bundled, so it looks the same offline.

- **Brief tab:** your SimBrief flight plan (route, time, fuel, weights) and, for the departure, arrival and alternate airports, the current METAR (decoded: wind, visibility, cloud, temperature, QNH, VFR/MVFR/IFR/LIFR) and TAF. Look up any airport by ICAO code. Weather is cached for 5 minutes. Enter your SimBrief name or pilot ID, and (for the coming Hoppie messaging) your Hoppie logon code, in the **VBuddy window** on the PC; they are saved on the PC only and never go to the relay or a phone.

- **ACARS (Hoppie)** in the Comms tab: send telex, answer CPDLC messages (WILCO / UNABLE / STANDBY, AFFIRM / NEGATIVE, ROGER), request a pre-departure clearance filled in from your SimBrief plan, request METAR / TAF / ATIS, and request a CPDLC logon. The bridge checks for new messages about once a minute while the simulator is connected, and a new message is pushed to your devices if Messages notifications are on. Needs your Hoppie logon code (VBuddy window, Your accounts).
- **Fix (2):** the Fenix's AP1/AP2 lights now also switch the autopilot flag in the live data on the bridge, so every device shows "Autopilot on" without depending on the Cockpit data reaching the app.
- **Discovery tool:** type a label (for example `DIR`) and press Enter, then press that key in the cockpit; the report lists what changed under each label. For finding MCDU keys later.
- **Fix:** the Flight tab showed "Autopilot off" on the Fenix. It now also reads the Fenix's AP1/AP2 lights, shows an Autothrust chip, and takes the selected speed, heading, altitude and vertical speed from the Fenix FCU.
- **Simulator and systems card:** pause/resume, gear, spoilers, parking brake, engines, APU, pitot heat, seat belts, flaps, time rate, altimeter and camera, each checked against the simulator. **Pause later** at a set time or before the top of descent, kept on the PC.
- **iFly buttons and knobs:** buttons are now pressed and released (a momentary press), and a knob first tries the aircraft's own "set" control before stepping it up or down, with wider name matching.
- **PMDG 737 / 777 switched on:** driven from the aircraft's own SDK data (found in its package folder, or copied into the `sdk` folder). PMDG liveries titled without the maker name (for example "737-800 PAX BW SC") are now recognised. The discovery tool skips GSX and camera-tool variables and has `--lvar-filter`.
- **Autopilot controls for add-on aircraft:** the Cockpit tab now shows an autopilot panel for every aircraft, not only the Fenix. PMDG 737 knobs use PMDG's own SDK events, FlyByWire A32NX / A380X use its FCU events, and iFly 737 MAX uses its own input events. Each change is checked and reported honestly if the aircraft ignores it. PMDG buttons are still to come.
- **Top of descent notice:** a phone notification (5 to 30 minutes before, default 10; chosen per device) that the descent should start soon, worked out from your SimBrief plan and your ground speed.
- **Autopilot-off notice (optional):** tick "Autopilot switches off in the cruise" in the notification settings to get a phone notification and a banner if the autopilot drops out above 3,000 ft above ground after being on for 30 seconds or more. Off by default; separate from Aircraft alerts.
- **Aircraft alerts** (phone notification and a banner in the open app): engine stopped in flight, stall warning, overspeed, gear up while low and descending, high sink rate below 1,000 ft, and low fuel. The fuel warning time is chosen per device (15 to 90 minutes, or off); it is worked out from how fast the fuel has really been burning over the last few minutes.
- **Notification settings** per device: ATC boundary warning distance (10 to 150 nm), an optional hourly limit on boundary and no-ATC notices ("Contact" calls always come through), and a **VATSIM connected / disconnected** notice.

- **Bridge** for MSFS 2024 (SimConnect) with a built-in test flight; serves the web app on your network. Runs as a notification-area (tray) icon with Open, Copy address, Pairing code, Settings, Log, Start with Windows, Restart and Exit.
- **Flight** dashboard, **Map** with live VATSIM traffic and ATC boundary alerts, **Landings** log with touchdown rate, G, flare and bounces.
- **Cockpit**: Fenix A320 FCU and EFIS with split-touch knobs, lights card for every aircraft. Other Airbus: lights only. Cockpit controls are Airbus-only in this release.
- **Comms**: Zulu clock, VATSIM status, COM1/COM2 tuning and listen toggles, transponder, chat (radio, private, broadcast, SELCAL), unread badge and chime, via the **VBuddy Link** vPilot plugin. "Listen on this device" opens VATSIM Listen.
- **Pairing**: viewing is open on your network; controlling needs a code from the bridge PC.
- **VBuddy window** on the bridge PC (opens from the tray icon): simulator and vPilot status, phone address with QR code, pairing code, paired devices, and the cloud relay switch.
- **Away from home**: a shared cloud relay (https, `vbuddy.duckdns.org`) that anyone can switch on in the VBuddy window with no setup: each install makes its own id and key, so many PCs share one relay and nothing secret is built in. Phone link with QR code. Or host your own. Phone notifications for ATC, landings and chat.
- **Flight log** on disk with automatic catch-up after a dropped connection.
- Windows installer (bundles Node.js), portable zip, optional Start-with-Windows and firewall rule.
- Tools: `aircraft:discover` (find an aircraft's controls), `fenix:discover`, `pmdg:dump`.

Known limits: not yet tested end-to-end against a real MSFS 2024, vPilot or relay VM by the author's automated tests; IVAO is not included; PMDG, iniBuilds, iFly and FSLabs cockpit panels are not available yet (the PMDG code is present but switched off).
